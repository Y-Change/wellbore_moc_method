# Neural-operator / PINN prior-art audit (2026-09-05)

This note records only claims that were checked against DOI metadata and/or an openly accessible abstract. A result on a wave, Burgers, shallow-water, or pipe-network benchmark is not evidence that the method has solved a wellbore with multiple perforation/fracture impedance jumps.

## 1. Training pathologies in PINNs: verified evidence and scope

* **Wang, Teng & Perdikaris (2021), SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 43(5), A3055-A3081, DOI [10.1137/20M1318043](https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1318043), arXiv:2001.04536, “Understanding and mitigating gradient flow pathologies in physics-informed neural networks.”** The abstract identifies numerical stiffness and unbalanced back-propagated gradients between composite loss terms as a failure mode; gradient-statistics learning-rate annealing and a more resilient architecture improved reported benchmark accuracy by 50–100x. This is an optimization diagnosis, not a theorem that every hyperbolic/high-frequency PINN fails. The paper does not model water-hammer friction memory or internal fracture junctions.

* **Wang, Yu & Perdikaris (2022), Journal of Computational Physics 449, 110768, DOI [10.1016/j.jcp.2021.110768](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2021.110768), arXiv:2007.14527, “When and why PINNs fail to train: A neural tangent kernel perspective.”** They derive the PINN NTK in the infinite-width limit and show different loss components can converge at markedly different rates; NTK-eigenvalue calibration is proposed. This supports a precise “gradient/conditioning imbalance” statement. It does not establish instability of a trained operator under impedance jumps or out-of-distribution fracture layouts.

* **Wang, Sankaran & Perdikaris (2024), Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering, DOI [10.1016/j.cma.2024.116813](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2024.116813), arXiv:2203.07404, “Respecting causality for training physics-informed neural networks.”** The published title omits “is all you need” (that wording is in the 2022 preprint). The abstract attributes errors in multi-scale/chaotic/turbulent dynamics to failure to respect spatio-temporal causality and introduces causal loss weighting, demonstrated on Lorenz, Kuramoto-Sivashinsky and turbulent Navier–Stokes. Causal weighting is therefore a mitigation with benchmark evidence, not proof that high-frequency water-hammer PINNs are impossible.

* **Wang, Wang & Perdikaris (2021), CMAME, DOI [10.1016/j.cma.2021.113938](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2021.113938), arXiv:2012.10047, “On the eigenvector bias of Fourier feature networks: From regression to solving multi-scale PDEs with physics-informed neural networks.”** The abstract explicitly reports conventional PINNs struggling with high-frequency/multi-scale targets and demonstrates spatio-temporal/multi-scale random Fourier features on wave propagation and reaction-diffusion, including forward and inverse tasks. A literature review must not claim that no PINN can represent water-hammer frequencies; the defensible gap is robust training and calibrated inversion under discontinuous, dissipative internal boundaries.

## 2. Neural operators and PINO: what has actually been shown

* **Kovachki et al. (2023), JMLR 24(89):1–97, “Neural Operator: Learning Maps Between Function Spaces With Applications to PDEs.”** Open JMLR page: https://jmlr.org/papers/v24/21-1524.html . The paper proves a universal approximation result for continuous nonlinear operators and discretization invariance; examples include Burgers, Darcy and Navier–Stokes. The page reports several-orders speedups over conventional solvers. The theorem assumes continuity of the target operator; a map whose input includes variable-location, high-contrast perforation/fracture jumps is outside that direct guarantee unless the interface geometry is represented in the function space and the resulting map is continuous in the chosen topology.

* **Li et al. (2020), arXiv:2010.08895, “Fourier Neural Operator for Parametric Partial Differential Equations.”** Open abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08895 . FNO learns a parametric PDE solution map via a Fourier-space integral kernel; experiments cover Burgers, Darcy and Navier–Stokes, with turbulent-flow zero-shot super-resolution and up to three-orders speedup. These are periodic/regular-grid PDE demonstrations, not perforation-junction scattering or non-stationary friction-memory identification.

* **Lu, Jin, Pang, Zhang & Karniadakis (2021), Nature Machine Intelligence 3:218–229, DOI [10.1038/s42256-021-00302-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-021-00302-5), “Learning nonlinear operators via DeepONet based on the universal approximation theorem of operators.”** DeepONet is an operator approximator, but universal approximation concerns continuous operators and does not ensure shock/interface fidelity. 

* **Wang, Wang & Perdikaris (2021), Science Advances 7(40), DOI [10.1126/sciadv.abi8605](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abi8605), arXiv:2103.10974, “Learning the solution operator of parametric partial differential equations with physics-informed DeepONets.”** The abstract says DeepONets normally require expensive paired input/output data and may violate physical principles; soft PDE penalties reduce data needs, even allowing PDE solution without paired observations, with a claim of O(10^3) time-dependent PDE predictions in a fraction of a second. The result uses soft penalties and standard PDE benchmarks; it does not enforce exact mass/energy balance at a perforation node or identify fracture impedance from one sparse pressure trace.

* **Li et al. (2024), ACM/IMS Journal of Data Science 1(3):1–27, DOI [10.1145/3648506](https://doi.org/10.1145/3648506), “Physics-Informed Neural Operator for Learning Partial Differential Equations.”** This is the formal PINO publication (the earlier 2021 arXiv:2111.03794 is a preprint). PINO combines coarse training data with higher-resolution PDE residuals, reports zero-shot super-resolution, and can work with PDE constraints alone on examples such as Kolmogorov flow. The abstract does not claim discontinuity-aware interface losses or water-hammer/fracture validation. “PDE residual at high resolution” is not equivalent to a Rankine-Hugoniot/perforation-node jump residual.

* **Rosofsky, Al Majed & Huerta (2023), Machine Learning: Science and Technology, DOI [10.1088/2632-2153/acd168](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-2153/acd168), “Applications of physics informed neural operators.”** Their benchmark includes 1D wave, 1D/2D Burgers and 2D shallow-water equations; source code and data are public. This is useful evidence that PINO can represent wave-like hyperbolic fields, while also delimiting the gap: no variable-location internal impedance, perforation discharge law, or nonlocal Zielke/Brunone friction state is included.

## 3. Discontinuity and conservation evidence that narrows (rather than erases) the gap

* **Lanthaler, Molinaro, Hadorn & Mishra (2022), arXiv:2210.01074, “Nonlinear Reconstruction for Operator Learning of PDEs with Discontinuities.”** Open abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01074 . For advection, inviscid Burgers and compressible Euler, they prove lower approximation bounds showing linear-reconstruction methods (including standard DeepONet/PCA-Net) cannot efficiently approximate discontinuous solution operators; nonlinear reconstruction (FNO and shift-DeepONet) can overcome those bounds. This is the strongest direct prior art against an unqualified “operator learning cannot handle discontinuities” claim. It still concerns solution discontinuities in benchmark conservation laws, not a one-dimensional acoustic water-hammer network with localized algebraic perforation losses and dynamic fracture storage.

* **Liu et al. (2024), ICML 41, PMLR 235:30965–30997, arXiv:2312.11176, “Harnessing the Power of Neural Operators with Automatically Encoded Conservation Laws.”** Open abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11176 . clawNOs use divergence-free predictions to automatically satisfy a continuity equation and report gains in small-data regimes. This establishes that exact/architectural conservation encoding is feasible. It does not encode compressible 1D mass plus momentum with source/friction terms, nor node-wise flow partition and pressure-loss laws.

* **Jagtap, Kharazmi & Karniadakis (2020), CMAME, DOI [10.1016/j.cma.2020.113028](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2020.113028), “Conservative physics-informed neural networks on discrete domains for conservation laws: Applications to forward and inverse problems.”** Conservative PINN is prior art for discrete conservation-law residuals and inverse problems. The existence of this method means “PINN has no conservation-aware formulation” is false; the unresolved issue is coupling conservative residuals to moving/unknown internal impedance interfaces and memory friction.

* **Hu, Jagtap, Karniadakis & Kawaguchi (2022), SIAM JSC, DOI [10.1137/21M1447039](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1447039), “When Do Extended Physics-Informed Neural Networks (XPINNs) Improve Generalization?”** Their theory identifies a decomposition trade-off: subdomains simplify each target, but fewer samples per subdomain can cause overfitting and worse generalization. XPINN therefore supplies a principled interface/domain-decomposition tool, while leaving unknown fracture locations, jump amplitudes and sparse inverse identifiability open.

* **Wu, Zhu, Tang & Lu (2023), preprint DOI [10.2139/ssrn.4583272](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4583272), “Solving Parametric Elliptic Interface Problems Via Interfaced Operator Network.”** This is evidence that interface-specialized operator architectures are emerging, but it is an SSRN preprint and elliptic, not hyperbolic water hammer. It should be cited as adjacent prior art, never as proof of a solved multi-fracture transient problem.

## 4. Water-hammer-specific correction (published before the survey cutoff)

* **Li, Ren & Wang (2026), Water 18(15):1900, DOI [10.3390/w18151900](https://doi.org/10.3390/w18151900), “Gradient-Based Equation Adaptive Weighting in Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Water Hammer Analysis.”** Crossref abstract verified 2026-08-04. GEAW-PINN dynamically weights governing-equation losses, uses classical water-hammer equations with the Brunone model, treats a model coefficient as trainable for simultaneous pressure/velocity prediction and inversion, and tests reservoir–pipeline–valve and pipe-network systems, including 25 dB noise. Reported single-system relative errors are 0.00742 (pressure) and 0.0183 (velocity). This paper closes the claim that “no PINN has addressed Brunone water hammer.” Its scope remains pipeline/valve networks generated from high-fidelity numerical reference data; the abstract gives no multi-cluster perforation/fracture compliance, unknown interface-location inversion, or neural-operator family generalization. It therefore strengthens, rather than removes, the case for a physically exact wellbore–multi-fracture operator.

## 5. Defensible research-gap wording

1. **Interface operator representation:** Existing discontinuity theory (Lanthaler et al.) concerns discontinuous PDE solutions and proves limitations of linear reconstruction; interface/operator papers are elliptic or geometry-specific. No verified paper above learns the coupled map with multiple unknown-location perforation nodes, algebraic discharge loss, and dynamic fracture storage in a compressible water-hammer model.
2. **High-frequency dissipative training:** Fourier features, causal PINNs, NTK calibration and GEAW-PINN each mitigate one training pathology. None demonstrates simultaneous high-frequency multiple-reflection fidelity with nonlocal/frequency-dependent friction memory and exact node jump residuals across OOD cluster layouts.
3. **Sparse inverse identifiability:** Physics-informed DeepONet and GEAW-PINN show fast forward prediction or coefficient inversion in benchmark settings, but neither establishes identifiability of several fracture openings/conductances/compliances from a single sparse wellhead trace. A new method must report posterior/uncertainty or sensitivity/rank analysis, not only low waveform error.
4. **Conservation and generalization:** clawNO/CPINN encode selected conservation laws; XPINN decomposes domains with a known trade-off. Compressible mass-momentum conservation with friction/source terms and node-wise flow partition remains unverified for a neural operator under OOD geometry and operating conditions.
