ABSTRACT
Many transient-based techniques have been developed for leak detection in piping ﬂuid systems, and they are thought to use leak-induced damping and
reﬂection effects to detect and locate the problem. This work investigates the relative importance of the damping and reﬂective effects from the leak to
the leak detection methods with the importance of both effects tested using four representative methods, which vary signiﬁcantly in their modes of
operation and provide the coverage of the major developments in the transient leak detection ﬁeld. It was found that all transient-based methods are
unable to detect and locate the leak in the absence of the leak-reﬂected signal, whereas the leak is detected and located even in the absence of leak-
induced damping. The results indicate that the information previously thought to be contained in the damping of slow transients was found to be
low frequency reﬂections from the leak.
Keywords: Inverse transient analysis, leak detection, pipe system, system response function, transient damping, transient reﬂection
1 Introduction
Compared with many commercial leak detection techniques, the
application of transient-based methods has advantages in terms
of their speed, ability to work online and large operational
range (Wang 2002). A leak in a pipeline system will result in
an increased transient damping rate and the creation of new
leak-reﬂected signals within the time trace. Many transient-
based leak detection methods have been developed and applied
to water piping systems using information contained within
these two effects. The developed leak detection methods vary
greatly in their modes of operation, but may be divided into
four main categories, namely the transient reﬂection method
(TRM), the transient damping method (TDM), the system
response function method (SRFM) and the inverse transient
method (ITM). It was found through recent ﬁeld studies that
all transient-based leak detection methods face difﬁculties in
systems with a high level of complexities. A better understanding
of their dependency on the different types of information con-
tained within the transient trace is essential when designing
new transient signals for improving their accuracy.
In general, all current transient-based leak detection methods
are unable to deal with systems with a high level of complexity
(practical ﬁeld level), and the understanding of their depen-
dency on the different types of information contained within
the transient trace is essential for improving their accuracy. A
recent literature review of Colombo et al. (2009) indicates
that the validations of the present transient-based leak detection

accuracy obtained if both the leak damping and reﬂection infor-

damping information appears to have little impact on the oper-
ation or the accuracy of the leak detection methods.

illustrated by comparing the transient information utilized in
TDM and the “leak-induced pattern” used in SRFM. The FRFs

5 Practical implications
From the analysis, it is concluded that all transient leak detection
methods have greater dependence on the presence of leak reﬂec-
tion information. In practical applications, the design of transient
signals for fault detections with clear reﬂections is ideal for this
technology. In the past decades, numerous researchers have
worked with different types of transients for condition monitor-
ing, from the usage of pump trips to inline valve closures and to
recent solenoid valves (Liou and Tian 1995, Brunone 1999,
Souza et al. 2000, Tang et al. 2000, Lee et al. 2006). Those
efforts found that the transients generated using the ﬁrst two
methods are generally slow and will create only dispersed reﬂec-
tions that are difﬁcult to identify, whereas the latter is fast and can
give clear reﬂections but is more difﬁcult to set up as additional
devices need to be installed to existing pipes. From the present
ﬁndings, the fast method for generating transients is preferable
for transient-based leak detection.

6 Conclusions
This research investigates the impacts of two leak-induced infor-
mation, damping and reﬂection, and their relative importance to
the application of present transient-based leak detection
methods. Four representative types of leak detection methods,
namely, the TRM, the TDM, the system reﬂection function-
based method and the ITM, were applied and discussed.
The results indicate that all transient-based methods are
unable to detect and locate the leak in the absence of the leak-
reﬂected signal, whereas the leak is detected and located even
in the absence of leak-induced damping. The analysis of this
study demonstrates that all techniques hinge on the presence of
the leak-reﬂected signal. Moreover, the information previously
thought to be contained in the damping of slow transients are
in fact low frequency reﬂections from the leak and are essential
to the leak detection process for all methods. The ﬁndings of this
study have implications on the design of transient signals for
fault detection and suggest that high bandwidth signals with
clear reﬂections are ideal for this technology. Meanwhile, the
understanding and deﬁnition of system reﬂection properties
can promote the practical applications of transient-based leak
detection methods.
