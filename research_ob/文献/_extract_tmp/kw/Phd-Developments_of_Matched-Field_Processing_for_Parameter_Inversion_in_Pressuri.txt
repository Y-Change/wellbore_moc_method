p.28: 28 ===== 1.2.1 Inverse Problem Framework for Parameter Estimation Using Hydraulic Transient Waves Accurate estimation of the unknown pipeline parameters can be achieved by measuring and ana- lyzing physical signals that are influenced by these parameters. For example, pressure head and flow discharge within pipelines are known to be affected by pipe dimensions, pipe defects and pipe wall material properties. In recent years, hydraulic transient waves (also known as water hammer waves or hydraulic transients), historically associated with destructive water hammer phe- nomena, are increasingly recognized in the literature as a promising tool for parameter estimation in pipelines. These transient-based methods offer advantages such as scalability to large systems, cost- effectiveness, and non-disruptive operations [30]. Transient waves can be generated in pipelines through intentional (active) or unintentional (passive) flow perturbations or disturbances, such as starting/stopping a pump, adjusting valves [31, 32], or using specialized devices like the Portable Pressure Wave Maker in large-diameter pipes [33]. Given the established role of transient waves in encoding pipeline parameters, our next step is to extract/infer these parameters from transient signals. Such task of determining the unknown pa- rameters from observed data is typically, in many scientific and engineering disciplines, formulated as an inverse problem. Specifically, our goal is to infer unknown parameters using the system’s transient response and a transient wave propagation model. However, this inverse problem is in- herently ill-posed as the exact solution cannot be directly obtained owing to the complexity of transient wave propagation (thus introducing model inaccuracies) and the inevitable presence of measurement noise. Consequently, advanced inversion techniques (or optimization methods) are required to obtain an estimated solution. To address this challenge, a promising way is the adop- tion of matched-field processing (MFP), a powerful inversion approach widely used in fields such as underwater acoustics for source localization and environmental characterization [39].
