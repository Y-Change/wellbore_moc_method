ABSTRACT
Horizontal well staged fracturing method is a pivotal technology in the exploitation of unconventional oil and gas 
reservoirs. During the pump shut-in process, the water-hammer wave generated, which propagates along the 
wellbore and reflects at the fluid entry point and bridge plug. Pressure oscillation signals are observable at the 
wellhead during pump shut-in phase, which encapsulate information about the reflection period of the water 
hammer wave. By analyzing the pressure oscillation signal at the wellhead, it is possible to ascertain the depth of 
fluid entry in the process of hydraulic fracturing. In this paper, the water-hammer wave propagation model of 
wellbore and hydraulic fractures system is established and validated by using the field data. The relationship 
between wave velocity and reflection time is studied, finding the water hammer signal is a periodic resonance in 
the frequency domain. By picking up resonance frequency, the wave reflection period can also be obtained. In 
this paper, a novel frequency difference method (FDM) is proposed to calculate the reflection time in the fre -
quency domain. The depth of the fracturing fluid entry point is determined by combining the echo time and wave 
velocity calculations. In this paper, field data from two field fracturing wells is collected. 10 staged water- 
hammer of wellhead pressure data are analyzed. The fluid entry point calculation results are consistent with 
the field results, which correlation coefficient is 99.6%. The results indicate the effectiveness of the proposed 
method. This paper provides a strong technical reference for determine fracturing fluid entry depth and hydraulic 
fracturing monitoring technology.
1. Introduction
Oil and gas are playing an irreplaceable role in promoting the 
economy, ensuring national security, and improving people ’ s living 
standards ( Zou et al., 2024 ; Yang et al., 2023 ). However, with the 
gradual depletion of conventional oil and gas resources and the 
increasing environmental problems, the development of unconventional 
oil and gas reservoirs has become particularly important. The increase in 
unconventional oil and gas production is due to the application of 
large-scale horizontal well staged fracturing technology ( Yang et al., 
2023 ; Zou et al., 2016 ), which forms a complex fracture ( Liu et al., 2023 ; 
Saberhosseini et al., 2019 ). In the staged fracturing of horizontal wells, 
hydraulic fracturing can provide channels for underground oil and gas 
flow.
With the increase of the buried depth of the reservoir, the plasticity 
of the reservoir is enhanced, and the soil stress and its difference in -
crease. In order to enhance oil recovery of the reservoir, it is necessary to 
add dense fractures. The number of clusters in a single segment is 
gradually increased from 2 to 3 clusters to 12 – 18 clusters. With the in -
crease in the number of clusters, the clusters do not fully open in the 
actual fracturing process. The productivity of each cluster varies greatly. 
Whether these tightly-spacing fractures can be effectively created under 
the condition of strong stress interference remains unclear ( Zhang et al., 
2021 ). At present, fracturing fracture detection technology is difficult to 
evaluate the effectiveness of fracturing parameters economically and 
quickly, which limits the closed-loop optimization of fracturing effect.
The depth of fluid entry point can evaluate fluid entry different in the 
multi-cluster hydraulic fracturing. In addition, the depth of the fluid

The detection methods for the depth of the fluid entry point in 
fracturing treatment mainly include micro-seismic monitoring technol -
ogy ( Hison et al., 2005 ; Wang et al., 2024 ), optical fiber sensing tech -
nology ( Holley and Kalia, 2015 ; Kalia et al., 2014 ), tracer technology 
( Kosynkin and Alaskar, 2016 ), acoustic or ultrasonic monitoring tech -
nology ( Lee et al., 1994 ; Zhou et al., 2023 ; Yang et al., 2023 ) and so on. 
However, the above methods have some limitations, such as downhole 
operations risking, high equipment costs and susceptibility to external 
disturbances. For example ( Lee et al., 1994 ), it is recognized that the 
micro-seismic energy is relatively small in their research. If the fractured 
reservoir is deep, the propagation of micro-seismic wave is obstructed, 
making it difficult for detectors arranged on the ground to receive micro 
seismic event. Holley and Kalia(2015) investigated the increase in 
fracturing production and found that ensuring the integrity of the fiber 
optic sensing system is the technical basis for all successful monitoring. 
Once the fiber optic is damaged in the wellbore, subsequent measure -
ments will be affected by unpredictable serious consequences and even 
fail completely. This method depends large surface area observation 
system. Du and Guan, 2005 used over sixty kinds of oilfield tracers, the 
statistical results showed that chemical tracers such as nitrate and po -
tassium iodide, were the most commonly used in field. Tang (2004) used 
acoustic logging data to process the imaging of out of well qualities, 
provided imaging examples, and preliminarily proved the feasibility of 
this technology.
It is worth noting that there is often a water-hammer effect during 
the fracturing treatment. At the end of each stage of fracturing con -
struction, a sudden change in wellhead displacement will generate a 
water-hammer pressure wave in the wellbore and propagates along the 
wellbore. When it reaches underground hydraulic fracturing, a portion 
of the energy of wave is reflected back to the wellhead. By installing a 
high-frequency pressure signal acquisition device at the well head, the 
water-hammer pressure wave signal is collected during hydraulic frac -
turing. The water-hammer signal generated by pump shut-in contains 
rich underground information. By identifying and analyzing the fluc -
tuation characteristics of wave signals, it can be used to evaluate the 
fluid entry position and scale of underground fractures ( Hu et al., 2022 ). 
The method of diagnosing fractures based on water-hammer signals 
effectively utilizes the its effect formed during fracturing pump shut-in. 
This fracture diagnosis technology based water hammer is easy to 
operate, cost-effective, and has strong timeliness ( Bogdan et al., 2016 ; 
Rutzinger et al., 2017 ). Specifically, after the staged fracturing and 
pump shut-in in a horizontal well, surface pressure acquisition equip -
ment can record pressure fluctuations caused by water-hammer effects. 
By analyzing the propagation characteristics, such as velocity, reflec -
tion, and attenuation, the position, even size. The morphology of frac -
tures can be identified ( Klem et al., 2017 ; Dung et al., 2021 ; Carey et al., 
2016 ). Dung et al. (2021) used water-hammer pressure data collected of 
the wellhead to obtain the pressure wave period, and studied the cor -
relation between water-hammer characteristics and fracture detection 
results by assuming reasonable boundary conditions. In addition, Shen 
et al., (2023) studied the correlation between water-hammer signals and 
fracture characteristics, and found that due to the uncertainty of 
water-hammer velocity, inaccurate understanding of the propagation 
speed of water-hammer wave in the wellbore may lead to misjudgment 
of hydraulic fractures. In the actual process, the wave velocity is greatly 
affected by the parameters of the well, and reasonable calibration of the 
wave velocity is necessary. However, the above research results assume 
that the wave velocity is constant, and the research on the 
water-hammer wave has not been conducted. This means that the above 
methods may be limited in practical fracture diagnosis applications.
At present, some researchers have used specific characteristics in 
water-hammer wave signals to determine the position of entry points 
( Hu et al., 2022 ; Ghidaoui et al., 2005 ; Parkhonyuk et al., 2018 ), and 
proposed analytical and numerical analysis methods for solving 
water-hammer differential equations. Hu et al. (2022) found that during 
field fracturing operations, the collected water-hammer signals gener -
ated by pump shut-in contain a large amount of noise. It is necessary to 
filter and denoise the water-hammer pressure signal in order to better 
analyze the fracture characteristics. Cao et al. (2019) solved the 
water-hammer equation using the method of characteristics and studied 
the effects of pump shut-in time and fracturing displacement on the 
water-hammer wave velocity. Similarly, Carey et al. (2015 , 2016) and 
Hwang et al. (2017) discussed pressure signals on the wellhead and 
fracture detection results of horizontal fracturing, equivalently pro -
cessing fracture parameters with circuits element. By adjusting the 
model parameters to match historical data, appropriate parameter 
ranges were obtained, providing reference parameters for fracture 
detection. To further increase the accuracy of numerical simulation, Ma 
et al. (2019) did not adopt the idea of equivalent circuit parameters of 
hydraulic fracture, directly considered perforation and fracture internal 
friction as boundary conditions. It is worth noting that in the above 
research results, most conclusions have made boundary assumptions in 
numerical calculations. When establishing a horizontal wellbore 
water-hammer model, the boundary conditions at the wellbore fracture 
bottom bridge plug node have been simplified, assuming that the frac -
ture is located at the bottom of the wellbore. However, this simplified 
assumption may lead to computational errors in the model. In addition, 
the above results mainly focus on the analysis of the propagation char -
acteristics of water-hammer wave in the wellbore, and there is no 
further analysis of the detection of entry point position. It should be 
pointed out that in actual fracturing analysis, the detection of the entry 
point position is one of the important parameters of horizontal well 
fracture diagnosis.
The main motivation of this article is to solve the detection of staged 
fluid entry points in horizontal wells based on wellhead pressure signal. 
The main innovations of this article are listed as follows: (a) The hori -
zontal well wellbore fluid entry point bridge plug fracturing pump shut- 
in water-hammer model is constructed. Based on the transient flow 
theory, the characteristic line method is utilized to solve the model. By 
verifying the field-collected wellhead pressure data, the accuracy of the 
established model is demonstrated. On this basis, the influence of 
different factors on water-hammer response was analyzed, which pro -
vides a basis for fluid entry point detection. (b) The time-frequency 
domain characteristics of water-hammer signals are analyzed. An algo -
rithm for locating the fracturing fluid entry point position is proposed, 
which uses the periodic method to calculate the wave velocity and the 
frequency difference method to calculate the echo time. By comparing 
the field application with the positioning results of the service provider’s 
fluid entry point, the accuracy of the positioning method proposed in 
this paper is verified. (c) The process flow of locating the fluid point at 
the fracturing field is established, which can provide more accurate in -
formation on the entry point position under complex geological condi -
tions, and provide important reference value for the evaluation of field 
hydraulic fracturing effects.
2. Methodology
In this section, we derive the fundamental equation of water-hammer 
effect based on the theory of transient flow. A mathematical model of 
water-hammer effect in horizontal well is established, which is solved by 
the method of characteristics. This paper adopts the equivalent circuit 
method to accurately describe the hydraulic fracture boundary in the 
horizontal well. The proposed model is then verified by field data. 
Finally, the influence of various factors, including entry point position 
and wave velocity on the model, are explored.

2.1. Physical model of water-hammer
When the pump shut-in, the fluid velocity within the string will 
change rapidly due to the incomprehensibility and high inertia of the 
fluid. This rapid change produces sudden variations in stage treatment 
pressure, which is transmitted in the form of pressure wave. This char -
acteristic is referred as water-hammer effect. The water-hammer occurs 
when the mechanical shock waves are generated at the fracture point in 
the bottom and the surface pressure sensor, leading to repetitive oscil -
lations between the two points. The wellhead monitoring device cap -
tures the water-hammer pressure signal and analyzes it to determine the 
location of the fluid entry point. In this study, a physical model of water- 
hammer is presented, as illustrated in Fig. 1 .
The fluid entry point refers to the precise position of the fracturing 
fluid underground. During multi-cluster fracturing operations, each 
cluster exhibits varying liquid inflows at this time, with the main fluid 
entry point identified as the cluster receiving the highest inflow. From 
this position, the critical information can be derived. 
(1) Through assessing the primary fluid entry point ’ s depth, the 
length from the fracture to the wellhead can be calculated. This 
assessment allows for evaluating the efficacy of the fracturing 
treatment, thereby providing a field method for the fracture 
monitoring.
(2) By locating of the primary fluid entry point, the effectiveness of 
the bridge plug can be further assessed, enabling the identifica -
tion of bridge plug movement and whether it has been properly 
set.
(3) Analyzing the fluid entry point enables optimizing the temporary 
plugging parameters, assessing the effectiveness of diverting 
methods, and achieving balanced initiation of multi-cluster 
fractures.
2.2. Water-hammer model assumptions
In each stage, the horizontal well multistage fracturing technology 
employs high-pressure fracturing apparatus to facilitate fracture prop -
agation. The water-hammer effect initiated during the pump shut-in 
stage causes the pressure fluctuations within the well and fractures. 
The attenuation speed and duration of this effect depend on the fluid 
entry point. Current water-hammer models predominantly rely on the 
assumption of a constant pressure boundary, which fails to accurately 
depict the water-hammer characteristics within horizontal wells during 
the pump shut-in phase of fracturing treatment. This study aims to 
develop a mathematical model of water-hammer. Prior to presenting our 
primary findings, several assumptions are established. (a)The wellbore 
is modeled as a one-dimensional propagation; (b) The fluid within the 
wellbore is incomprehensible; (c) Physical properties, pressure and ve -
locity are distributed at each point across the same cross-section; (d) The 
diameter of each part within the wellbore is constant, neglecting energy 
loss at the connection part; (e) The wellbore interacts with the formation 
exclusively through fractures; (f) The inclination of the horizontal sec -
tion of the horizontal well remains constant.

3. Characteristic analysis of water-hammer effect
The water-hammer pressure wave produced by a fractured pump 
shut-in exhibits distinct characteristics in both the time domain and 
frequency domain. In the time domain, one can calculate the wave ve -
locity based on the reflection period. Meanwhile, in the frequency 
domain, the location of the fluid entry point is determined by the fre -
quency of the fundamental and the harmonic, i.e., the proposed fre -
quency difference method in this study.
3.1. Time domain characteristic
In this subsection, the time domain analysis of water-hammer signals 
is mainly used to determine the wave velocity of pressure wave.
3.1.1. The influence of wave velocity
The influence of wave velocity on the internal water-hammer effect 
within the wellbore is vital, as it directly impacts both the magnitude 
and extend of the water-hammer effect, as well as its potential impact on 
the wellbore structure and fracture. Typically, wave velocity is governed 
by the fluid ’ s comprehensibility and density inside the wellbore. In 
geological medium, the wave velocity is also influenced by the elastic 
modulus and density of the medium ( Song et al., 2023 ). The precision of 
the wave velocity significantly affects the detection of the fluid entry 
point.
In this study, the wave velocity is assumed to be 1600 m/s, 1400 m/s, 
1200 m/s and 1100 m/s, respectively. The impact of the wave velocity 
on the water-hammer signal is investigated, then different pressure-time 
curves are obtained, as shown in Fig. 8 . The findings demonstrate that 
the larger the amplitude of water-hammer oscillation is, the more 
obvious the water-hammer effect is. The greater the amplitude of the 
water-hammer oscillation, the more pronounced the water-hammer ef -
fect, with a smaller oscillation period and slower attenuation. In 
contrast, at a water-hammer wave velocity of 1100 m/s, compared to 
the previous three wave velocities, the is a delayed reflection of the 
water-hammer signal, reduced oscillation amplitude a notable oscilla -
tion period, and a quicker rate of attenuation.
The pump shut-in water-hammer signal curve recorded during the 
fracturing stage treatment is illustrated in Fig. 9 . A and C in Fig. 9
constitute one cycle of water-hammer. The wellhead can be regarded as 
a closed boundary for the transmission of the water-hammer wave in the 
wellbore, while the bottom hole fracture boundary acts as an open 
boundary. The B , and C moments denote the reflection of the expansion 
and the compression waves, respectively. The calculation for the 
reflection period T of the water-hammer pressure wave is outlined as 
follows T = 4 L / a ( Hu et al., 2023 ), where L represents the distance from 
the wellhead to the bottom hole water-hammer reflection position, a 
signifies the water-hammer pressure wave velocity.
Based on the water-hammer model established above, this paper uses 
the periodic method to calculate the wave velocity, which is deduced as 
a = 4 L / T .

and the wellhead affects the water-hammer wave. The selected distance 
between the perforation site and the wellhead were set at 5130m,

Currently, the frequency difference method for locating liquid entry 
depth still has some limitations, which may be attributed to the 
following factors. First, the field water hammer signal is prone to noise 
interference, which affects wave velocity extraction and identification. 
Second, a low sampling frequency or inadequate signal processing res -
olution may result in the loss of critical details. Additionally, the pre -
cision and calibration of the sensors and equipment used for signal 
acquisition can affect signal quality and measurement accuracy.
To minimize these errors, the following measures can be imple -
mented. Proposing more efficient water hammer signal noise removal 
techniques, which is also the direction the author intends to pursue in 
future research. Improving the existing wellhead pressure acquisition 
equipment to increase the sampling frequency of the water hammer 
signal at the wellhead. Selecting pressure sensors with faster response 
times, ensuring that the response time is kept within 1 ms.
5.7. Compared with cepstrum analysis
The two-dimensional cepstrum analysis requires segmenting the 
pressure signal by using a sliding window to compute the one- 
dimensional cepstrum within each window, and then stitching them 
together to obtain the two-dimensional cepstrum image( Hu et al., 
2022 ). Furthermore, The two-dimensional cepstrum analysis can be 
broken down into three steps, as shown in Fig. 20 .
This paper performs a cepstral analysis on water hammer data 
collected from stage 17 of well 1. Due to the negative impedance at the 
fracture, the fluid entry time corresponds to the larger negative 
impedance values in the time-domain cepstrum plot. According to 
Fig. 21 , it can be observed that the dark blue band indicates the position 
where the negative cepstral value is the smallest, and the reflection time 
of the pressure wave at the fracture is 2.61s, In section 5.3 , the wave 
velocity was calculated to be 1531.1 m/s, from which the fluid entry 
point depth was determined to be 3996.2 m. The error compared to the 
result calculated using the frequency difference method is 0.6 m, which 
proves that the method for calculating the fluid entry point depth pro -
posed in this paper is accurate and simple.
The two-dimensional cepstral method analyzes water hammer sig -
nals to extract their periodic and frequency characteristics, providing 
high time-frequency resolution and strong adaptability. However, it 
requires extensive frequency domain transformations and complex 
signal processing during localization, which results in a high computa -
tional cost that may limit its real-time application. Additionally, the 
method demands high-quality raw signal data and is sensitive to pa -
rameters such as window size and overlap ratio. The selection of these 
parameters is often influenced by the experience of field engineer. 
Incorrect parameter settings of cepstral method can lead to significant 
errors, so precise tuning is essential to ensure accuracy.
In comparison, the frequency difference method determines the time 
and location of fluid arrival at the fracture by analyzing changes in 
frequency response. Compared to the two-dimensional cepstrum 
method, this approach is computationally simpler, requires fewer re -
sources, is less influenced by variations in personnel experience, offers 
better real-time performance, and can quickly diagnose fracture loca -
tions, providing real-time guidance for optimizing field fracturing 
operations.
