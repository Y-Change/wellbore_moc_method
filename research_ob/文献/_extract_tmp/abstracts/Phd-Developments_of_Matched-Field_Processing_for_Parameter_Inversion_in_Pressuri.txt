ABSTRACT
This thesis develops an innovative inverse analysis framework for accurate determination of defect
characteristics and material properties of pressurized water supply systems (WSSs) based on the
matched-field processing (MFP) methodology—a signal processing approach widely and success-
fully used in other domains such as underwater acoustics for source localization and environmental
characterization. The MFP methodology involves (i) an experimental step where transient waves,
propagating at high speed (km/s), are introduced into the pipe system and the response is sensed;
and (ii) a theoretical step where the measured response is “matched” with the replica response (de-
rived from the mathematical model of waves in pipes). The proposed MFP framework is found to
be (i) super-resolving; thus, beating the diffraction limit and enabling each defect in a cluster of
ii\n===== PAGE 3 =====\ndensely (defect-to-defect distance is less than half of the minimum probing wavelength) spaced de-
fects to be individually and clearly detected; (ii) able to significantly suppress unwanted side-lobes;
thus, addressing the issue of ambiguous main-lobe identification; (iii) robust and stable against
various noise types, including white, colored, and impulse noise, even under low signal-to-noise
ratio conditions; and (iv) accurate. These substantial advantages are due to the following three
attributes. First, MFP fully leverages both amplitude and phase information of the broadband tran-
sient signals. Second, MFP is statistically optimal under Gaussian noise; thus, ideally suited for
WSSs since laboratory and field data show that the noise in these systems is well described by
the Gaussian probability distribution. Third, a novel understanding of the mechanism responsible
for the development of side-lobes is acquired in this research, resulting in approaches to deal with
these unwanted phenomena in parameter estimation in WSSs. The proposed MFP methodology
is validated through comprehensive simulation and experimental studies in three specific applica-
tions: determination of location and size of a single leak; determination of location and size of
clustered leaks where the leak-to-leak distance is smaller than half a wavelength; and determina-
tion of the viscoelastic parameters of our testing facilities. The MFP method is generalizable across
pipeline systems, applicable to all types of unknown parameters, rapid to perform, non-intrusive,
non-destructive, and supports real-time health condition assessment of WSSs.
