Physics-informed neural network model for transient wave propagation in a 
pressurized pipeline 
 
 
Muhammad Waqar (1), Moez Louati (2), Sen Li (3), and Mohamed S. Ghidaoui (4) 
 
(1,2,3,4) Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering, The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, Hong Kong, SAR

Abstract 
 
This paper explores the use of neural networks (NNs) to model water-hammer waves propagation in a bounded 
pipe system. The training dataset is obtained from a numerical solution using the Method Of Characteristics 
(MOC). It is found that conventional NNs fail to model the water-hammer waves propagation when trained using 
only the boundary conditions and require large datasets for training. However, the use of physics-informed NN 
(PINN), wherein the one-dimensional wave equation, along with the boundary conditions are imposed as part 
of the loss function , provides accurate results. The PINN model parameters (i.e., weights and  biases) are 
optimized using (i) the Adaptive Moment Estimation (A DAM), and (ii) the limited -memory Broyden -Fletcher-
Goldfarb-Shanno (L-BFGS) algorithms. The results show that L-BFGS based solution is in good agreement with 
the exact MOC solution, whereas the ADAM-based solution has slow convergence . Moreover, it is found that 
the PINN solution is sensitive to changes in boundary and initial conditions as well as to the time length of the 
wavefield solution. In particular, our results show that as the time length (or bandwidth) of the signals increases, 
a denser NN model architecture (in terms of number of neurons) is required to accurately predict the response. 
It is also found that the PINN model is sensitive to the location of additional measurement point s used as a 
constraining dataset. Detailed sensitivity analysis concerning model architecture and the constraining dataset 
is yet to be investigated. The advantages and implications of using PINN in hydraulic transient modeling are 
discussed. 
 
 
Keywords: Artificial intelligence; Leak detection; Hydraulics; Machine learning; Water-hammer 
 
 
1. INTRODUCTION 
 
 Recently, Raissi et al., (2019) proposed an elegant framework, named physics-informed neural networks 
(PINNs), to predict the solution of differential equations using neural networks (NNs). As the name implies, this 
approach unifies data -driven machine lea rning and domain knowledge to optimize the machine learning (ML) 
model. PINNs have gained significant attention in the scientific community and particularly in computational 
society due to their remarkable ability to predict solutions for PDEs. Another substantial advantage of PINN is 
that this approach requires significantly less data for training in comparison to the conventional NN models due 
to the fact that physical laws are enforced as part of the loss function in the optimization procedure (Karniadakis 
et al., 2021). Raissi et al., (2019) demonstrated the strength of PINNs by finding the solutions for the Burger’s 
equation, 1D Schrödinger equation, and Allen -Cahn equation. Waheed et al., (2021) used PINNs to so lve the 
eikonal equation to predict the seismic wave travel times for seismological applications. Lai et al., (2021) used 
the PINN framework to solve the ODEs for structural identification applications using numerical as well as 
experimental data. Huang et  al., (2021) extended the PINN framework to account for the point sources in the
