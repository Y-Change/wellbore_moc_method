Abstract 
 
This paper explores the use of neural networks (NNs) to model water-hammer waves propagation in a bounded 
pipe system. The training dataset is obtained from a numerical solution using the Method Of Characteristics 
(MOC). It is found that conventional NNs fail to model the water-hammer waves propagation when trained using 
only the boundary conditions and require large datasets for training. However, the use of physics-informed NN 
(PINN), wherein the one-dimensional wave equation, along with the boundary conditions are imposed as part 
of the loss function , provides accurate results. The PINN model parameters (i.e., weights and  biases) are 
optimized using (i) the Adaptive Moment Estimation (A DAM), and (ii) the limited -memory Broyden -Fletcher-
Goldfarb-Shanno (L-BFGS) algorithms. The results show that L-BFGS based solution is in good agreement with 
the exact MOC solution, whereas the ADAM-based solution has slow convergence . Moreover, it is found that 
the PINN solution is sensitive to changes in boundary and initial conditions as well as to the time length of the 
wavefield solution. In particular, our results show that as the time length (or bandwidth) of the signals increases, 
a denser NN model architecture (in terms of number of neurons) is required to accurately predict the response. 
It is also found that the PINN model is sensitive to the location of additional measurement point s used as a 
constraining dataset. Detailed sensitivity analysis concerning model architecture and the constraining dataset 
is yet to be investigated. The advantages and implications of using PINN in hydraulic transient modeling are 
discussed. 
 
 
Keywords: Artificial intelligence; Leak detection; Hydraulics; Machine learning; Water-hammer 
 
 
1. INTRODUCTION 
 
 Recently, Raissi et al., (2019) proposed an elegant framework, named physics-informed neural networks 
(PINNs), to predict the solution of differential equations using neural networks (NNs). As the name implies, this 
approach unifies data -driven machine lea rning and domain knowledge to optimize the machine learning (ML) 
model. PINNs have gained significant attention in the scientific community and particularly in computational 
society due to their remarkable ability to predict solutions for PDEs. Another substantial advantage of PINN is 
that this approach requires significantly less data for training in comparison to the conventional NN models due 
to the fact that physical laws are enforced as part of the loss function in the optimization procedure (Karniadakis 
et al., 2021). Raissi et al., (2019) demonstrated the strength of PINNs by finding the solutions for the Burger’s 
equation, 1D Schrödinger equation, and Allen -Cahn equation. Waheed et al., (2021) used PINNs to so lve the 
eikonal equation to predict the seismic wave travel times for seismological applications. Lai et al., (2021) used 
the PINN framework to solve the ODEs for structural identification applications using numerical as well as 
experimental data. Huang et  al., (2021) extended the PINN framework to account for the point sources in the 
PDEs.  
 Motivated by these  recent intriguing development s of PINN , this work uses PINN for water hammer  
application and discusses its potential and possible use in practice. Unlike other systems of interest (e.g., open-
channel flows, or structures), pipeline systems pose different and unique challenges. That is, these systems are 
underground, hence poorly accessible , have partially known  topologies, and are comprised of dynamic 
boundary conditions (BCs). Under these constraints, the adoption of the PINN framework to predict the transient 
response of buried pipe systems is not a straightforward task. For instance, it is impossible to measure the initial 
conditions (ICs) continuously along the pipe in a functioning or operating  pipe system. This paper shows that 
additional measurement points can compensate for the missing IC constraint. In what follows, the methodology 
is presented at first. Then, the numerical examples are provided . In particular, the solution of the PINN is 
compared with conventional NN and discussed. The sensitivity of the PINN model concerning the measurement

3.1.1 Performance comparison between ADAM and L-BFGS algorithms 
 
Figure 3 gives the loss functions of both optimizers and shows that L-BFGS has a faster convergence rate 
in comparison to ADAM. In terms of computational cost, the ADAM optimizer was completed in about 3 hours 
and 30 minutes, while for the same number of iterations (i.e., for 30×103 iterations), L-BFGS took around 4 hours 
and 54 minutes to complete. Studies suggest that ADAM optimizer with a mini-batch approach requires more 
epochs (i.e., more iterations) to achieve effective results which may increase the computational cost  Raissi et 
al., (2019) . However, as known for any machine learning model, these suggestions are highly 
problem/application dependent. An appealing approach to reduce the computational cost is to adopt the transfer 
learning approach suggested by Raissi et al., (2019) wherein a pre-optimized model can be used to re-optimize 
over a new dataset. 
In terms of accuracy, the results  obtained with each optimizer are presented in Figure 4. The intensity of 
the color in this figure shows the amplitude of the non-dimensional pressure head. Figure 4(a) displays the exact 
MOC solution. Figure 4(b) shows that L-BFGS has accurately predicted the transient response of the system at 
all time steps. In comparison, Figure 4(c) shows that the ADAM optimizer has roughly captured the overall wave 
path and needs more iterations to further refine the model parameters. Similar observations are also noticed by 
other researchers in this area ( e.g., Alkhalifah et al., (2021); Tartakovsky et al., (2020); Raissi et al., (2019)).  
Therefore, in what follows, only L-BFGS algorithm is used.

of layers and number of neurons ). Such discrepancies can be minimized in several ways  by: (i) increasing the 
number of layers  as increasing the number of layers helps to predict the sharp er functions (Alkhalifah et al.,

4. CONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE PLANS 
 
In this paper, the framework of a physics-informed neural network (PINN) is explored to predict the 
hydraulic transient response of a reservoir-pipe-reservoir system. The results of the PINN model are compared 
with the exact MOC solution and with the ordinary NN model. The results show that the PINN algorithm can 
predict the transient response of the system throughout the pipe domain. However, it is also  noticed that the 
accuracy of the model is sensitive to the (i) frequency bandwidth of the signals, (ii) time length of the solution, 
and (iii) spatial location of data points as constraints. In particular, the results demonstrate that as the length or 
frequency bandwidth of the signal increases, a denser PINN architecture is required to accurately predict the 
response of the system. Furthermore, i t is noticed that some measure ment locations bring more benefit s in 
terms of optimizing the model than others. More research is required to further elucidate the sensitivity of PINN 
models concerning constraining datasets and PINN architecture. Currently, the PINN framework  is being 
extended for the case where the effect of friction and fluid -structure interaction are included (e.g. , steady & 
unsteady frictions and viscoelastic effects) . Another potential application of PINN would be to predict the 
transient response of a pipe network which will be explored in future work.
