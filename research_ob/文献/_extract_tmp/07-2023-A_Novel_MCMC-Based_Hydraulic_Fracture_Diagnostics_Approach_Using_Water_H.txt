1. INTRODUCTION 
Hydraulic fracturing has been the commonly used 
stimulation technique for the economic development of 
shale/tight gas and oil reservoirs. Although rapid progress 
has been made in fracturing technology, accurate 
characterization of hydraulic fracture is a great challenge 
which hinders the evaluation of fracturing efficiency and 
prediction of well productivity.  
Various techniques have been developed to characterize 
hydraulic fractures, which can be divided into two 
categories, i.e., direct monitoring methods and indirect 
interpretation methods. Direct monitoring methods 
mainly include micro-seismic monitoring (Le Calvez et al. 
2007; Zhu et al. 2017), fiber-optic sensing ( Liu et al. 2020; 
Liu et al. 2023) , coring observation  (Maity et al. 2018), 
and downhole imaging (Roberts et al. 2018a; Roberts, et 
al. 2018b). These methods are usually characterized by 
their high accuracy, but limited by the high cost and 
limited observation range. Instead, indirect interpretation 
methods, including flowback analysis (Chen et al. 2018; 
Liu et al. 2019), pressure transient analysis (He et al. 2017; 
Wang et al. 2021), and water hammer analysis  (Carey et 
al. 2015; Hu et al. 2022) , are commonly used low -cost 
fracture diagnosis techniques. However, flowback 
analysis and pressure transient analysis usually require 
the production data after the well completion, which can 
hardly provide the real -time fracture information during 
hydraulic fracturing. The water hammer analysis has the 
advantages of short testing time, low-cost and real time.  
Water hammer refers to the pressure wave generated by 
the sudden change of fluid velocity when shutting down 
surface pump at the end of hydraulic fr acturing 
stimulation. This process can be affected by the friction 
along the wellbore, pressure loss through perforations, 
and leak-off into the fractured zone. Once the wellbore 
geometry and the fluid properties are known, the 
characteristics of water hammer signatures can reflect the 
properties of generated hydraulic fractures. Water 
hammer interpretation may be used as an inexpensive and 
efficient diagnostic technique for the characterization of 
hydraulic fracture geometry. Efforts have been paid to 
forward modeling of the water hammer signatures and 
infer the hydraulic fracture properties. Holzhausen and 
Gooch (1985) firstly introduced the water hammer 
signature into fracture diagnostics, and they used 
hydraulic impedance testing to analyze the water hammer 
data and characterize the fracture geometry. Mondal 
(2010) used an electrical circuit analogy to model the 
fracture related pre ssure drop. Carey et al. (2015) 
extended the model for multiple hydraulic fractures by 
lumping multiple fractures into an effective fracture. Ma 
et al.  (2019) proposed a new model to simulate the 
pressure transient in wellbore by incorporating the

ABSTRACT: The water hammer signatures, which can be observed after pump shutdown at the end of a hydraulic fracture treatment, 
have proven to contain useful information about the geometry of generated hydraulic fractures. This paper presents a novel hydraulic 
fracture diagnostics approach using the water hammer data. A water hammer model that describes the pressure transient in 
hydraulically fractured horizontal well is established. In the model, a new multi -cluster hydraulic fracture boundary condition is 
derived by considering the perforation friction and leak -off in fractures. The method of characteristics is applied to efficiently solve 
the water hammer model. The water hammer signals can be effectively reproduced under different fracture characteristics. T he 
MCMC method is applied to construct the posterior statistics of fracture parameters (e.g., width, length, height). The propos ed 
approach is first verified by a series of synthetic models, and then applied to a real field case of a multi -stage hydraulic-fractured 
horizontal well in a shale gas reservoir to demonstrate its applicability and efficiency.

pressure transient in hydraulically fractured horizontal

hydraulic fracture boundary condition is derived by 
considering the perforation friction and leak -off in 
fractures. The method of characteristics is ap plied to

Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method, which can

field cases are used to testify the performance of the 
proposed approach in hydraulic fracture characterization 
using water hammer data.

3.2. Real Field Case Study 
In this section, a real field case of a multi-stage hydraulic-
fractured horizontal well in a shale gas reservoir is used 
to demonstrate the applicability of the proposed method. 
The length of the vertical section of the wellbore is 33445 
m, and the length of the horizontal section is 1818 m. The 
horizontal is divided into 20 fracturing stages with the 
length of each stage being 90.9 m. 9 clusters for each stage 
and 7 perforations for each cluster are designed. One or 
two temporary plugging are applied for each stage to 
improve the fracturing efficiency. Then based on the 
water hammer data of each stage, the parameters of 
hydraulic fracture geometry are interpreted through the 
proposed approach.   
We take the fifth and sixth stages as examples to show the 
history matching results of water hammer data, as shown 
in Fig. 6.  Good agreements between simulated data and 
observed data of real field cases can be still achieved, 
which testify the performance of the proposed method in 
hydraulic fracture characterization. Besides, the 
interpretation results of fracture geometry for all stages 
are summarized in Fig. 7. The averaged fracture half -
length and height are 131.87 m and 25.8 m, respectively. 
Moreover, the fracture length and height become larger 
after plugging, which proves the effectiveness of plugging.

4. CONCLUSIONS 
In this paper, a novel hydraulic fracture diagnostics 
approach using the water hammer data  is presented . A 
water hammer model that describes the pressure transient 
in hydraulically fractured horizontal well is established by 
fully considering the effect of wellbore friction, 
perforation friction, and fluid leak -off in hydraulic  
fractures. A hydraulic fracture geometry interpretation 
approach is developed based on the MCMC method.  
The proposed approach is verified by a series of synthetic 
models. The results show that the parameters of fracture 
half-length, height, number of eff ective fractures, and 
number of opened perforations can be effectively 
identified. Besides, the introduction of prior information 
about the fracture geometry can improve the 
interpretation accuracy. 
Finally, a real field case of a multi -stage hydraulic -
fractured horizontal well in a shale gas reservoir is used 
to testify the applicability and efficiency of the proposed 
approach.
