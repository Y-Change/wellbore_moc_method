 A B S T R A C T
The detection of a cluster of leaks in pressurized water supply pipelines is crucial as it allows for early 
recognition of the pipeline’s poor overall condition, enabling timely interventions. This paper addresses this 
challenge by extending, for the first time, a high-resolution modified broadband matched-field processing 
(MB-MFP) framework to the multi-leak detection problem. The developed methods combine advanced signal 
processing with acoustic measurements to achieve super-resolution identification, where leaks spaced closer 
than half the minimum probing wavelength are successfully resolved. A key contribution is a methodology 
that enables the simultaneous estimation of the number, locations, and sizes of multiple leaks. We also show 
that the techniques are robust to various noise types and levels, including white noise with a signal-to-noise 
ratio (SNR) as low as −10 dB, as well as colored and impulse noise. The effectiveness and robustness of 
the proposed techniques are demonstrated through comprehensive numerical simulations and validated with 
laboratory experiments.
1. Introduction
Identification and localization of leaks in urban water supply sys-
tems (UWSS) can prevent water loss, reduce repair costs, and mitigate 
environmental impacts. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency 
(EPA) conducted a comprehensive assessment of existing leak detection 
technologies and concluded ‘‘None of the technologies was able to discern 
two separate leaks in close proximity . . . and appear only to report the 
larger leak rate for the cluster of leaks. The ability to identify whether 
a signal is from an isolated leak or multiple leaks in close proximity is 
helpful in judging the general condition of a pipeline’’ (Nestleroth et al., 
2012). In addition, a pipeline with clusters of defects (e.g., leaks) 
has typically lower load-bearing capacity than the same pipeline with 
individual defects (Zhang et al., 2023). Moreover, Lo et al. (2021) 
notes that the interacting defects are more detrimental towards the 
structural integrity of a pipeline than single defects. OMS Ltd (2025) 
also stated that if clustered defects appear and are left untreated, they 
can progressively spread over time, link together, and lead to a severe 
pipe failure. Therefore, there is a critical need to develop methods that 
can identify defects that are in close proximity.
In recent years, numerous technologies exist to detect leaks, exam-
ples include model-based (Waqar et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2025) and

In this paper, we seek to extend these methods to multi-leak de-
tection, especially for the cluster of leaks where the leaks are closely 
spaced. In the context of wave propagation, ‘‘closely spaced’’ refers to 
distances between consecutive leaks that are smaller than the diffrac-
tion limit, i.e., half the wavelength of the probing wave (Fink, 2006; 
Waqar et al., 2022). While our previous work established the founda-
tion for high-resolution single-leak localization, extending this frame-
work to multi-leak scenarios presents several new and non-trivial chal-
lenges. First, the measured pressure signal becomes a complex su-
perposition of responses from multiple leaks, and disentangling these 
overlapping signals to identify individual sources is a significant de-
convolution problem. Second, the search for 𝐾 candidate leaks must 
be performed in a high-dimensional parameter space, which dramat-
ically increases the computational complexity compared to the one-
dimensional search for a single leak. Third, The presence of multiple 
leaks inherently lowers the signal-to-noise ratio for each individual 
leak’s contribution. Distinguishing the complex, superimposed leak 
signal from sophisticated environmental noise (e.g., white or non-white 
noise) requires a significantly more robust methodology. Finally, and 
most critically, the true number of leaks (𝑃 ) is generally unknown in 
practice, necessitating a methodology that can estimate this number 
rather than assuming it a priori. This paper builds upon the foun-
dational MB-MFP technique to specifically address these challenges, 
providing a comprehensive solution for multi-leak detection.
The structure of this paper is as follows. Section 2 introduces the 
frequency-domain transient wave model for pipe systems and explains 
how the head difference signal, generated by leaks during bidirectional 
wave propagation, is extracted for detection. Section 3 details the 
proposed MB-MFP techniques for multi-leak localization. The perfor-
mance of these methods is then evaluated through comprehensive 
numerical simulations, demonstrating its accuracy and super-resolution 
capabilities under various noise conditions and for closely spaced leaks 
with different leak numbers. Section 4 investigates the sensitivity of 
the proposed methods when the assumed number of leaks in the model 
(𝐾) differs from the true number of leaks (𝑃 ), showing that for 𝐾 ≥ 𝑃 , 
the estimated locations reliably converge to the actual leak positions. 
Section 5 experimentally validates the MB-MFP techniques in a labo-
ratory setting featuring dual leaks of varying sizes. Finally, Section 6 
summarizes the key findings and provides concluding remarks.

4. A methodology to determine the unknown leak number
This section investigates a methodology to determine the unknown 
number of leaks, building upon the earlier discussion where the as-
sumption 𝐾 = 𝑃 was explored. In real-world applications, accurately 
knowing the number of leaks (𝑃 ) in advance is often impractical, which 
prompts the examination of scenarios where 𝐾 ≠ 𝑃 . Our analysis 
spans scenarios with 𝐾 < 𝑃 , 𝐾 = 𝑃 , and 𝐾 > 𝑃 . It is found that 
the 𝐾 estimated leak locations tend to cluster around the 𝑃 actual

property provides a systematic approach to concurrently estimating the

plete linear features. In pipeline systems, where the model is linearized 
to account for multiple leaks, this over-completeness holds true. This 
property not only aids in precise leak localization, but also facilitates 
the estimation of the number of leaks.

5. Experimental validation in a laboratory setup
This section presents the experimental validation of the proposed 
MB-MFP techniques in a controlled laboratory setup at the Water 
Resources Research Laboratory of HKUST. It is important to note that 
the objective of this experiment is not to replicate the exhaustive 
parametric study of the numerical simulations, but rather to serve as a 
proof-of-principle. The goal is to demonstrate that the key phenomena 
and the superior performance of the proposed methods, predicted 
under idealized numerical conditions, hold true in a real-world physical 
system. This system introduces inherent complexities, such as the vis-
coelastic behavior of the High-Density Polyethylene (HDPE) pipe wall 
(accounted for by the Kelvin–Voigt model in Eq. (4)), uncharacterized 
measurement noise, and non-ideal transient generation, thus providing 
a robust test of the methods’ practical applicability.
The facility comprises a pipeline system, illustrated through a com-
prehensive 3D schematic and a simplified 2D layout as shown in Fig. 
9. This pipeline system includes essential components such as a booster 
pump at the upstream end and a valve at the downstream end. These 
key components of the experimental setup are summarized in Table 
2. The experimental loop in Fig.  9(a) comprises straight pipe runs 
connected by long-radius bends and standard couplings. Bend angles 
range from 70◦ to 90◦ and radii of curvature (𝑅𝑐) from 0.20 m to 
0.30 m, chosen to limit form losses. In the steady state, minor losses 
due to fittings are accounted for by calibrating an effective Darcy–
Weisbach friction factor to match the measured total head loss. For 
the transients considered here, the injected signals are band-limited 
with a minimum wavelength 𝜆min = 𝑎0∕𝑓𝐽 ≈ 61 m  (the choice of the 
maximum frequency 𝑓𝐽 is based on the SNR of the measured signals 
along frequency), which is much larger than the characteristic bend

6. Conclusions
This study has presented a methodology using the modified broad-
band matched-field processing (MB-MFP) techniques to accurately and 
simultaneously estimate the number, sizes and locations of leaks in 
pressurized pipelines. The proposed techniques exhibit high efficiency 
and robustness against various noise types, including white, colored, 
and impulse noise. This robustness originates from the full utilization 
of the chosen-bandwidth signals and the normalization applied to the 
frequency-domain signals. Additionally, these techniques afford super-
resolution capability (i.e., enabling the detection of leaks spaced closer 
than 0.5𝜆min) and high-resolution estimation results. A key finding of 
our research is that when the number of leaks assumed in the model (𝐾) 
equals or exceeds the actual number of leaks (𝑃 ), the estimated leak lo-
cations reliably cluster around the real leak sites. This clustering effect 
facilitates not only the precise localization of leaks but also the accurate 
enumeration of them. The proposed methods are analyzed through

Future work is planned to focus on applying the MB-MFP tech-
niques for leak detection in real-field experiments and handling the 
relevant uncertainties. We are currently conducting fieldwork for 6 
discrete District Metering Areas (DMAs) of various scales to validate 
the methodology. The uncertainties include insufficient knowledge of 
the system’s structure and device behavior, and imprecise measure-
ment of wave speed, friction factor, and steady-state discharge, which 
may affect the accuracy of the leak localization. Moreover, the fun-
damental Matched-Field Processing (MFP) framework is inherently 
generalizable to more complex network topologies, such as one with 
branches, loops, or variable diameters. One would simply replace the 
single-pipe transfer matrix model used here with a more sophisticated 
network hydraulic model. The core MB-MFP and MB-MFP’ algorithms 
for matching, localization, and side-lobe suppression would remain the 
same. Therefore, the extension of our proposed MB-MFP techniques to 
complex networks is also a promising and logical direction for future 
research.
