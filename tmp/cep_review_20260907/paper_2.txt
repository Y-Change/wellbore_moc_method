ABSTRACT
Laser-induced surface acoustic wave (SAW)-driven nondestructive evaluation offers high-resolution, non-contact 
characterization of subsurface microstructures. However, its practical application is often limited by the high 
computational costs associated with traditional numerical simulation methods. Recently, machine learning has 
emerged as an attractive alternative to accelerate these simulations. In this paper, we develop a neural operator- 
enabled framework for both forward and inverse modeling of laser-induced SAW propagation. A general dataset 
with randomly generated subsurface structures is used to evaluate and quantify the model ’ s performance in both 
wave propagation and subsurface inversion problems. Three potential applications are then investigated: sub -
surface crack localization, multilayer structure characterization and polycrystalline grain imaging. The results 
demonstrate that the neural operator-enabled model achieves satisfactory accuracy even in the presence of noise 
and source waveform variations, underscoring its potential as an efficient and accurate surrogate model for 
practical nondestructive evaluation using laser-induced SAWs.
1. Introduction
Ultrasonic guided waves have attracted significant interest across a 
broad spectrum of fields, ranging from nondestructive evaluation and 
structural health monitoring of industrial systems ( Tian et al., 2019 ; 
Capineri and Bulletti, 2021 ; Yang et al., 2023 ) to fundamental research 
on various waveguide configurations ( Feng et al., 2024 ; Hess et al., 
2014 ; M. Liu et al., 2024 ; Wu et al., 2023 ) and advanced theoretical 
modeling methods ( Nicoli et al., 2022 ; X. Liu et al., 2024 ; Hua et al., 
2024 ; Liu et al., 2025 ). Acoustic waves can be guided by different types 
of boundaries, which ultimately determines their application scenarios. 
Surface acoustic waves (SAWs) ( Rayleigh, 1885 ) are guided waves that 
use the surface as a waveguide, confining elastic energy to the surface 
with a penetration depth that depends on the wavelength. This unique 
characteristic makes broadband SAWs particularly effective for char -
acterizing subsurface features of materials or structures. While SAWs are 
typically excited by piezoelectric transducers, advances in laser tech -
nology have enabled the rapid development of laser-induced SAWs, 
which offer significantly higher spatial resolution and enable 
non-contact measurement, leading to widespread applications ( Hess 
et al., 2014 ; Liu et al., 2022 ; L. Ding et al., 2023 ; Huang et al., 2023 ; 
Baher et al., 2024 ; Cheng et al., 2024 ; Jia et al., 2024 ; Zarei and Pilla, 
2024 ).
Research on laser-induced SAW-driven nondestructive evaluation is 
mainly based on analytical modal analysis ( Liu et al., 2022 ; Huang et al., 
2023 ; Jia et al., 2024 ; Baher et al., 2024 ) or numerical dynamic analysis 
( Cheng et al., 2024 ; L. Ding et al., 2023 ; Zarei and Pilla, 2024 ) of waves. 
Modal analysis, or more specifically dispersion analysis, is valued for its 
tractability of analysis and ease of implementation, but is often limited 
in the types of features that can be detected compared to numerical 
dynamics methods. Traditional numerical methods, such as the finite 
difference ( Liu et al., 2021a ), finite element ( Cheng et al., 2024 ; L. Ding 
et al., 2023 ; Zarei and Pilla, 2024 ) and spectral element ( Fiborek et al., 
2022 ) methods, simulate wave propagation in complex media by dis -
cretizing the elastic wave equation in time and space, approximating its 
derivatives using finite-difference formulas or basis-function expansions 
and associated parameters, and performing repeated forward model 
runs. Although these methods can achieve high solution accuracy, they 
usually incur significant computational cost, especially in inverse 
problems, which greatly limits their application in real-world nonde -
structive evaluation.
Thanks to significant advances in artificial intelligence, particularly 
in machine learning (ML), many researchers have begun exploring ML- 
enabled approaches to address wave propagation problems. A

solving a specific problem instance and struggle to generalize across 
different scenarios, such as changes in source characteristics or velocity 
structures, without re-optimization. This constraint hinders their effec -
tiveness as surrogate models for practical applications.
Neural operators have emerged as a paradigm for learning solution 
operators rather than solving individual physical systems. These neural 
networks treat both inputs and outputs as functions and operate directly 
on them. For wavefield prediction problems, neural operators learn the 
solution operators of a family of partial differential equations governing 
wave propagation. Specifically, the input can encompass various ma -
terial properties, source parameters, and initial and boundary condi -
tions, while the output represents the corresponding wavefield 
solutions, capturing both spatial and temporal information. Currently, 
the Fourier Neural Operator ( Li et al., 2021 ) and its variants, such as the 
U-shaped Neural Operator ( Rahman et al., 2023 ), the Group-equivariant 
Fourier Neural Operator ( Helwig et al., 2023 ) and the Factorized Fourier 
Neural Operator ( Tran et al., 2023 ), have been increasingly applied to 
such problems in geophysics ( Lehmann et al., 2023 , 2024 ; Yang et al., 
2021 ), gradually demonstrating superior performance.
In this paper, we aim to investigate the potential applications of 
neural operators in ultrasonic nondestructive evaluation. Specifically, 
we develop a framework for both forward and inverse modeling of laser- 
induced SAW propagation using neural operators. The remainder of this 
paper is organized as follows: Section 2 presents the methodology, 
including dataset generation, operator-enabled modeling and training 
strategies; Section 3 evaluates the performance of neural operators on a 
general example, where subsurface features are characterized by a 
random velocity distribution; in Section 4 , we apply the neural operator- 
enabled approach to tackle challenges encountered in practical laser- 
induced SAW-driven nondestructive evaluation; conclusions are finally 
presented in Section 5 .
2. Methods
This section aims to design neural operators for forward and inverse 
modeling of laser-induced SAWs, with a focus on uncovering the 
intrinsic relationships between SAW waveform evolution and subsurface 
material properties in a supervised learning setting. The designed op -
erators are intended to serve as highly accurate and efficient surrogates 
to traditional numerical solvers in applications of ultrasonic testing.

2.3. Neural operators for the forward and inverse problems
According to the simulation strategy detailed in Section 2.1 , the 
subsurface structure model, described by the shear wave velocity V
S
, has 
a size of 64 × 128, concatenated with two coordinate grids for x and z . 
The wavefield, represented by the vertical velocity V
Z

128 × 256, concatenated with the same coordinate grids and time t . To 
reduce computational cost and better align with practical scenarios of 
forward and inverse problems, only the surface wavefield is retained in 
this study. In this case, the forward problem is to predict the propagation 
of SAWs on a given subsurface structure excited by an equivalent laser 
source. The input a for the forward problem is a variable of size 64 ×
128 × 3, comprising three channels: the shear wave velocity V
S
, and the 
x and z coordinates. The output u is a variable of size 64 × 256 × 1, 
containing only the vertical velocity component V
Z 
as its single channel. 
In contrast, the inverse problem aims to estimate the subsurface struc -
ture from the observed SAW evolution. There are typically two ap -
proaches to solve this problem: one is the optimization-based inversion, 
which iteratively applies the forward operator by updating the struc -
tural parameters to minimize the constructed misfit function, similar to 
the full waveform inversion using traditional numerical solvers ( Rao 
et al., 2020 ; Aktharuzzaman et al., 2024 ; Wu et al., 2025 ); the other is to 
directly train a new inverse operator, which is simpler and more effi -
cient. Here we adopt the latter approach, where the input a has a format 
similar to that in the forward problem (64 × 256 × 3), consisting of V
Z
, x 
and t channels, and the output is V
S 
with a size of 64 × 128 × 1. The 
overall architecture of forward and inverse modeling is shown in Fig. 1
(b).

2.4. Training approach
After applying appropriate feature scaling to the simulated dataset, 
the neural operator-enabled model is trained in a supervised learning 
setting. The dataset is divided into three subsets: 80 % for training, 
which updates the neural operator parameters; 10 % for validation, 
which monitors performance and mitigates overfitting; and the 
remaining 10 % for testing, which evaluates the generalization capa -
bility of the model. The training process employs the Adam optimizer 
( Kingma and Ba, 2017 ) with hyperparameters β
1
= 0 . 9 and β
2
= 0 . 999 
and the initial learning rate of 0.001, augmented with dynamic learning 
rate scheduling, where the learning rate is halved after 10 epochs if the 
validation loss plateaus. Additionally, convergence-driven early termi -
nation is applied, with an early stopping patience of 60 epochs and a 
tolerance threshold of 0.0001. The relative L
1
-norm error serves as the 
default loss function. The batch size is set to 25, and the maximum 
number of training epochs is set to 500.

Fig. 3 presents the average results across all 200 test samples for the 
6-layer, 24-channel configuration. It is observed in Fig. 3 (a) and (b) that 
both the ToF error and cross-correlation coefficient degrade as the 
propagation distance increases, with average values of 1 . 575 Δ t and 
0.977, respectively. This deterioration is attributed to the increasing 
distortion, spreading and attenuation of the acquired SAW waveform 
due to scattering caused by the heterogeneous subsurface structure. The 
relative waveform error in the frequency domain, shown in Fig. 3 (c), 
demonstrates that the errors become more pronounced as the frequency 
increases, despite the fundamental frequency being 4 MHz. The x -axis 
range in Fig. 3 (c) corresponds to the effective bandwidth, defined by the 
1/e threshold of the peak amplitude. The results in Fig. 2 (d) and Fig. 3 (c) 
reflect the well-known spectral bias phenomenon, where neural net -
works tend to first learn low-frequency components corresponding to 
large-scale features ( Rahaman et al., 2019 ). Layer-wise evaluation re -
sults of the inverse operator in Fig. 3 (d) and (e) show better accuracy in 
the upper layer, with a mean correlation coefficient of 0.617 and ve -
locity error of 265.8 m/s, compared to the lower layer (0.214 and 417.8 
m/s, respectively). The upper-layer correlation degrades with propa -
gation distance due to the increasing complexity in explaining the 
far-field SAW waveform. In contrast, the other three quantitative results 
in the inversion do not exhibit clear correlations with propagation dis -
tance. Overall, despite a decline in the performance of both the forward 
and inverse operators with increasing propagation distance, the results 
remain within an acceptable range.
As with conventional neural network training, hyperparameters in -
fluence the performance of neural operators, with the number of chan -
nels and layers being particularly impactful on the prediction of elastic 
wave propagation ( Lehmann et al., 2024 ). We systematically evaluate 
10 pairs of forward and inverse operators with different numbers of 
channels and layers using the metrics defined in Eqs. (6) – (10) (e.g., 
ToF), and quantitatively compare them with the default configuration 
from Fig. 3 , as summarized in Fig. 4 . The results indicate that waveform 
prediction accuracy consistently improves with larger architectures, 
reaching an optimal balance at 24 layers and 6 channels. In contrast, the 
inverse operators primarily depend on channel capacity, while varia -
tions in layer depth result in only marginal fluctuations. To effectively 
resolve SAW evolution details and capture more features in the V
S 
profile, a high-channel configuration ( ≥ 24 channels) is necessary. 
Finally, the performance of the default forward and inverse configura -
tions (24 layers and 6 channels) is evaluated across the training, vali -
dation and test datasets using the ToF error and the cross-correlation 
coefficient as metrics. As shown in Fig. 5 , the results for the three 
datasets are comparable, indicating that the model in this study is not 
subject to overfitting.

4.1. Subsurface cracks

4.3. Polycrystalline materials
In this final case study, we address a particularly challenging appli -

S

Fig. 12 presents representative results in the test subset. The forward

5. Conclusions
This study presents a novel modeling approach enabled by neural 
operators for the forward and inverse simulation of laser-induced sur -
face acoustic wave (SAW) propagation in nondestructive testing. In our 
method, both the forward and inverse neural operators are constructed 
using uplift and projection layers in combination with factorized Fourier 
layers and trained in a supervised manner. The ground truth wavefield 
data, represented as a three-dimensional variable with two spatial and 
one temporal dimension, is obtained from numerical simulations of 
elastic wave propagation from a fixed equivalent laser source. To 
maintain computational efficiency and practical relevance, only the 
essential SAW field information is retained. The performance of the 
operators is evaluated using a random velocity dataset and some metrics 
such as the time-of-flight error, demonstrating their good generalization 
capabilities in this context.
Building on the promising validation results, the neural operator- 
enabled method is subsequently applied to representative laser- 
induced SAW-driven nondestructive evaluation scenarios, aiming to 
address key challenges in the field. Comprehensive assessments on 
subsurface crack localization, multilayer structure characterization and 
polycrystalline grain imaging demonstrate that the forward operator 
effectively captures the complex scattering and dispersion of SAW 
propagation, while the inverse operator reliably reconstructs velocity 
profiles reflecting microstructural features even in the presence of noise 
and source waveform variations. Although inherent spectral bias issues 
may exist within the neural operators, their overall performance re -
mains highly satisfactory. Moreover, the dispersion curve-based one- 
dimensional inverse operator achieves prediction errors comparable to 
full waveform inversion while significantly reducing training 
complexity, making it particularly well-suited for multilayer structures. 
These findings underscore the versatility, robustness and efficiency of 
neural operators in capturing complex wave dynamics and microstruc -
tural characteristics, thereby paving the way for their broader applica -
tion in practical nondestructive evaluation.
